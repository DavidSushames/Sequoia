Going over the foundations of the world, making sure everything makes some kind of sense.
# Creator and Sun
At the risk of sounding cliche:
In the beginning, there were two guys. One who wanted things to exist, and one who did not.
The good guy (probably) and the bad guy (definitely) had a great big fight about this, and it still goes on to this day.

The good guy, having much better PR, was called something like The Creator, or maybe The Authority. They created Sequoia and everything in it.
The bad guy, having not such good PR (the public tend to enjoy existence) was called something like The Adversary, The Void, or maybe That Bastard. They try their best to unmake Sequoia and everything in it.

Anyways, The Authority now is, or simply lives within, the Sun.
They keep things real.
# Void
Void is the guy who wanted things to not exist.
Things existing is irritating as FUCK to him, and as such, he wants to unmake it all.
The issue is that it's very hard to defeat something by using nothing, so he has to use *something*, even though it irritates him so.
At least he can use negative/anti/unreal somethings, which at least reduces the total amount of *something* in existence.
He's not very creative, as planning require processing power, which requires a thing, which he hates. He's pretty good at taking things that exist and corrupting them into a fucked up evil form though. 
Aberrations are based upon regular animals, except warped and corrupted by the unreality of the Void. 
Void souls are when a person's soul has been corrupted/replaced by an unreal equivalent. They are psychopathic killers who seek to inflict as much suffering upon the world as possible. Death is the goal, and pain is a joke. 
Void witches are something between a mage and a cleric, that have a small amount of void energy in their soul, mixed in with the world energy. They can use this to shape spells. Void witch dooms result in them become some awful kind of void horror.
# Mantles and Cultures
Aight, here's the big ol model of how things work.
Picture one of those diagrams that shows how a neural network works, except make it vertical (I hate AI as much as the next guy, but I don't have a better description for this.) 
At the top of model, there's the Sun. All powerful source of light, heat, and reality.
Below the sun, there are two layers, made up of a whole heap of nodes.
The first layer is made up of Mantle nodes, and the second is Culture nodes.
At the bottom of the model, there's the Void. A gap in space where nothingness resides. 

Mantles are all the various Concepts of the world. Not all of them are as simple as Fire, Lightning, Ice, etc. There are some real weird ones like Ant, Mother, Vampirism, or Turning Your Skeleton Into A Soultrap So That You Can Puppet Your Own Corpse After Death.
These concepts can be mantled by a creature, giving them cool powers and such, but each concept only has space for one user, so its first come first served. 
While mantles are all technically part of the same layer (mantles don't connect to other mantles), some are higher up and more powerful than others. The highest ones make you a god, while the lowest ones make you an immortal. You're still more powerful than any regular person though. Mantles are closer to the sun than Cultures, so you are more real than people, and are harder to kill. There's other Mantles between the god and immortal layers, where you find things like Dragons, Kaiju, and the equivalent of Gilgamesh, king of kings.

Culture nodes are all the various cultures of the world. Each culture has its own flavour of world energy, which determines things like what the people look like, the spirits in the area, and how the Mantles manifest. Cultures have connections to Mantles, based on the interpretation that culture has of that concept. Fire spirits around a tundra culture might be happy and warming, and aggressive and volatile in a forest culture, due to how the different cultures feel about fire. Likewise, the culture node of a tropical island tribe might have no connection to mantles such as Ice, Vampirism, or Bears.

Just because a Mantle is occupied, that doesn't mean that it can't be drawn upon by others.
Creatures who attempt or are made to connect to a mantle will either claim it if they are the first, or echo it if they are not.
This is how you get fire spirits everywhere despite the mantle of Fire being taken. The difference is like fireflies dancing around a lighthouse, but it's still there.
Echoing a mantle means that you have left your culture node, and are no longer a Person. Echoes are also more susceptible to Cultural interpretation. 
The Vampire was the first person to perform some dark ritual which gave him powers, and also the mantle. His house also had a bat infestation, which got tied up with his legend in the the local culture. Later people who performed the same ritual would end up echoing the mantle, and got bat associated abilities. If the Vampire moved to a new area and took up parkour, maybe the new culture would associate vampires with jumping. Either way, because they have left their culture node to hover around the mantle instead, lesser vampires are dangerous bloodsucking creatures, and are definitely not people. 
The first Zombie is a guy with cool undead strength and regeneration, but lesser zombies are mindless shamblers.

# Gods, Souls, and People
Gods are people/spirits/whatever who hold a powerful mantle. The highest mantles deal with Divine Energy and Faith.

All living creatures have souls. People's souls have a special shell made of divine energy, created by the gods, that prevents their soul from leaking out to their body, like how animals are. This (divine shell/golden core/whatever) is what makes you a person, capable of abstract/sapient thoughts, being part of a culture, and all that cool stuff that people can do that animals can't. 
It's also what the Void pulls on to make you fall if you step off the edge of the world, and to pull your soul down after you die.
Some people give up their golden shells for political reasons, and go live in the wild as apes.

If a culture node is a tree, then people are like leaves. They are tiny, numerous, and let the culture breathe and change. When they die, they fall off the tree and into the Void below. Unless of course, a god saves them and carries them into the sun, to become a Sun Snake.

A god's connection to a culture works like this:
The culture's world energy/faith feeds into the god, determining how its miracles manifest.
The Ant god might be Ant the Builder in one region, and Ant the Soldier in another.
The god sends divine energy through the connection to its faithful, doing whatever kind of miracles the world energy allows for. This only works if the culture actually worships the god, cultures that only worship god A can't recieve miracles from god B.

# The Soul Cycle
Souls aren't free to create, and the Void's attacks are relentless. If every soul fell into the Void after death, the sun and the gods would run out of power, the void's minions would encroach, and everyone would be fucked. The void takes the souls that it collects, and forges them into Voidbeasts. Giant black awful space monsters that fly towards Sequoia to wreak havoc, bite chunks out of the world, etc.

Luckily, the gods can rescue faithful souls after their death, and ferry them into the sun to be with others of their culture. When a particular culture reaches critical mass of souls, it coalesces into a Sun Snake, and emerges from the sun. "Newborn" snakes are smaller, and fly along the topside of the world, gathering strength for when they make it to the edge and go underneath to battle the Voidbeasts below. Very cool and cinematic space fights between snakes and beasts.
Even so, energy loop is still losing power through unfaithful souls and defeated sun snakes. This is remedied by victorious sun snakes dragging back voidbeast carcasses back into the sun where they can be harvested for energy. The balance is tenuous however, and if too many snakes die, or not enough souls are faithful, the world will be in trouble.

The gods know this, and this is where they get a little immoral. Gods know that an easy life does not lend itself to faith, and nothing inspires prayer like a bit of danger. "There's no atheists in a foxhole" and all that. So the gods decide to shake things up every 500 or so years, to keep people on edge and keep the population nice and religious. Imagine how things like a day/night cycle, seasons, or animals being twice the size would cause trouble if they didn't exist previously. The first winter, your crops die, and everyone starves. "Oh lord, save us!" the people cry.   
It's all a wee bit fucked up, but ultimately necessary for the continuation of the world.

Oh yeah, sun snakes are made of sun, so when they die and fall to the ground, they melt a big ol' hole. Volcanoes. There's way more of them on the underside, as the actual battles take place down there, but there's still a couple on the surface.
# Reality Spectrum 
Everything exists on a spectrum of how real it is, where their magnitude determines how difficult they are to affect.

| Score | Object                    |
| ----- | ------------------------- |
| 1     | The Sun, The Authority    |
| 0.9   | Gods                      |
| 0.8   | Sun Snakes                |
| 0.7   | Souls, Hyperreal Biomes   |
| 0.6   | Immortals                 |
| 0.5   | People, Animals, Rocks    |
| 0.3   | Subreal Biomes            |
| 0     | Nothing. Vacuum of space. |
| -0.5  | Aberrations               |
| -0.7  | Void Souls                |
| -0.8  | Void Beasts               |
One might expect the Void itself to be -1, but it actually exists outside of the spectrum. The Void it is not in of itself unreal, it merely fights using unreality. Still, it is as powerful as if it *did* have a score of ±1.


The sun emits light, heat, and reality. Light lets you see things. Heat means you don't freeze. Reality enforces shape and definition to the Void. Sequoia exists in a Goldilocks zone between the two forces. 
Sequoia's Bedrock is hyperreal, which keeps the underground stable while reality shadows exist on the surface.
Sunlight will harm aberrations, as they are unreal flesh being annihilated by the hyperreality of the sun. Void souls are unaffected, as they are an unreal soul housed within real flesh.

# World Energy
World energy is the most common form of energy. It flows through everything, and is locally "coloured" by cultures in the area.
As a person's soul fills up with a culture's world energy, it starts to affect their physical form. Those who embrace a culture start to take on physical traits of that culture. An arboreal culture might have long limbs for climbing and a better sense of balance, while a desert culture might be better as regulating their body temperature. You have to actually adopt the culture, it's not just a proximity thing. Once you switch to the new culture node, you'll start taking in the local world energy, slowly replacing that of your old culture. So "that weird outsider guy" will stay the same until he starts participating in cultural norms and such.

World energy also forms into spirits, which naturally echo various mantles that the culture has understandings of. See the Mantles and Cultures section for how tundra and forest cultures interpret Fire. However, this only affects their generation. If a tundra mage has a fire spirit companion and then travels to the forest, the spirit won't suddenly get all volatile.

If a culture dies via natural disaster, genocide, etc. the dead culture's world energy will remain until it is replaced by a new culture's colour, continuing to form spirits based on the dead culture. This has potential for "this was built on an indian burial ground" type plots, as well as why the Appalachians are so spooky.
